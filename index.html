Judaism
Parashat Lech-Lecha — Genesis 12:1–17:27; Haftarah: Isaiah 40:27–41:16
Today

Parashat Lech-Lecha spans Genesis 12:1–17:27: God tells Abram to leave his father's house for a land he has not yet seen, promising to make him into a great nation, and Abram sets out with his wife Sarai and nephew Lot, passing through Canaan and a famine-driven detour to Egypt before settling near Hebron. After Lot splits off to live near Sodom and is later rescued from a war among regional kings, God formalizes a covenant with Abram: countless descendants and the land itself, paired with a warning that his offspring will first spend centuries enslaved in a foreign country before any of it is realized. The portion closes with Abram, now ninety-nine, undergoing circumcision as the covenant's physical sign and being renamed Abraham, while Sarai becomes Sarah and both are told, despite their age, that they will have a son. The haftarah, from Isaiah, speaks to that promise from the other end of history: comforting an exhausted, exiled Israel by reminding them that the same God who once called an obscure, childless man out of his homeland and built a nation from him has not forgotten what became of that promise, and has the same strength now to renew them.

Core principle

A promise that asks for sacrifice up front and pays off only generations later keeps its credibility by becoming, for a discouraged people long afterward, concrete proof that this kind of promise can hold.

What's a commitment in your life that asked you to give up something familiar now, with no clear sense of when — or whether — you'd ever see it pay off?

How to read this page. Each entry names its source the way that tradition cites itself — chapter and verse, a portion's name, a lectionary date — so the full text is easy to find elsewhere. The summary and principle are written in plain, non-devotional language on purpose: not a claim about what's true, just what the passage says and what it's asking of a reader. One entry unlocks a day; the page is rewritten daily and the week turns over each Monday.
