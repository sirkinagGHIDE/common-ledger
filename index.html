Judaism
Parashat Toldot — Genesis 25:19–28:9; Haftarah: Malachi 1:1–2:7
Today

Parashat Toldot covers Genesis 25:19–28:9. After Isaac prays for his barren wife Rebecca, she conceives twins who struggle violently inside her, and an oracle tells her the elder will serve the younger; grown, Esau sells that birthright to Jacob for a bowl of stew, and the text notes flatly that he "despised" it. Decades later, with Isaac old and nearly blind, Rebecca coaches Jacob to impersonate his brother so he receives the blessing meant for the firstborn — a deception Esau discovers only after it's done, which sends him vowing revenge while Jacob flees to his uncle Laban's household. The haftarah, from Malachi 1:1–2:7, has God affirming love for Jacob over Esau, then turning that same unsparing standard on the priests of Malachi's own day for offering blemished, careless sacrifices instead of the honor their position demands.

Core principle

Something treated as disposable by the person who holds it doesn't stay unclaimed for long — it passes to whoever is willing to actually value it, and the deal is already final by the time the one who gave it up wants it back.

Where in your own life have you shrugged off something valuable because having it felt like a given, the way Esau treated his birthright — and what would it take to notice its worth before someone else does?

How to read this page. Each entry names its source the way that tradition cites itself — chapter and verse, a portion's name, a lectionary date — so the full text is easy to find elsewhere. The summary and principle are written in plain, non-devotional language on purpose: not a claim about what's true, just what the passage says and what it's asking of a reader. One entry unlocks a day; the page is rewritten daily and the week turns over each Monday.
